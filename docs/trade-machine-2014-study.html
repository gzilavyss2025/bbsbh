The 2014 ESPN Trade Machine, and what to borrow

Studied from the Wayback Machine copy of espn.go.com/nba/tradeMachine dated 2014-01-01. The building screen still runs there. The result screen didn't: the call that returns the verdict was never archived. Its wording, colours and layout come from the page's own archived code. I found no working shared-trade link in the archive. The 2011 and 2016 copies weren't checked.

How it worked

Building
  • Teams are columns side by side, each headed in club colour with "Cap Room" and "Over Tax Line".
  • Clicking a player moves him into the column of the team that gets him, under an "Acquiring 1 Player $12,000,000" bar.
  • His old roster row darkens with "Being Traded To Boston Celtics".
  • Players who can't be traded are greyed out with a ⊘ and the rule ("A free agent can not be traded until December 15…").
  • One centred "Try This Trade" button.
Result
  • One banner on top: "This Trade is Successful!" on green, or "This Trade Failed" on red with each team's reasons.
  • The rosters hide; each column keeps only what that team acquires.
  • Each column gets "Hollinger's Analysis: +3 Wins" and a sentence: "With this trade you have increased this team's projected wins by 3."
  • A footnote explains the method.
Sharing
  • "Save Trade" gives a link that reopens straight into the result, with only "New Trade" on offer.

Why it felt easy

  • One click per player, and the column is the destination.
  • Totals update live.
  • Bad picks are greyed out before you try them, not after.
  • The answer is one banner, and the result screen hides everything that isn't the deal.

Recommendation

Build the first three: "gets" columns, the factual banner and the one sentence per club. They're the parts that made the Trade Machine readable in two seconds, and none of them adds a value judgment.
