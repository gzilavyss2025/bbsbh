Trade Workbench: what other trade tools do

Research for a page where you pick players from two clubs' rosters and look at a possible trade · 2026-11-18

The short answer: every baseball trade tool turns the question into one money number and a verdict saying who wins. Fans still say the verdicts are wrong. The NBA and NHL tools succeed because they check real rules, such as whether the salaries match. No tool in any sport handles a trade that has been reported but is not yet on the transaction wire. Tally can be the trade tool that shows the facts (who each club controls, for how long, at what cost, and the roster consequences) without grading the trade, and it can be the first to track a reported trade until it becomes official.

Marks: CONFIRMED = checked in a web search this session. UNCONFIRMED = from background knowledge and not re-checked; details may be out of date. No tool page was opened directly. Check the key tools by hand before you build.

2 · Patterns across every sport

  1. Rules engine vs. value engine. NBA and NHL tools answer "is this legal?" MLB has no salary cap, so every MLB tool answers "is this fair?", which is an opinion. (The labor deal expires December 2026, and fans expect a fight over a salary cap CONFIRMED.)
  2. One number per player plus a balance bar plus a verdict is the standard screen.
  3. The top complaint everywhere: "the model accepts absurd trades." Users want the verdict explained, or no verdict at all.
  4. Share links and images drive growth. Fanspo's counter-offer link turns one trade into a conversation.
  5. In baseball, years of control are the column that matters, ahead of age and prospect rank.
  6. Data freshness wins. ESPN lost ground to Fanspo by falling behind rule changes.
  7. Legacy tools were built for desktop; newer ones work on phones. UNCONFIRMED

3 · Baseball details nobody models

None of these tools appear to show any of the following UNCONFIRMED. Each one is a fact, not an opinion, so each fits Tally's tone:

  • Room on the 40-man roster after the trade (does a club have to cut someone?)
  • Option years left, and Rule 5 eligibility for prospects
  • No-trade clauses and 10-and-5 rights (the player can block the deal)
  • Competitive balance picks, the only MLB draft picks a club can trade
  • Payroll before and after against the luxury-tax (CBT) thresholds
  • Cash considerations, and players to be named later (PTBNL)

5 · What I recommend

Build a "trade card," not a trade grader. Pick two clubs, tap players from each roster, and the page lays out both sides like a scorebook ledger. For each player it shows age, position, salary this year, years of control, WAR (last season and 3-year), and prospect rank. Totals sit at the bottom: WAR moved, salary moved, years of control moved, 40-man room after the trade, and any no-trade or 10-and-5 flags. No "who wins" verdict. That avoids the complaint every value tool gets, and it matches the app's facts-first voice.
  • Version 1: two clubs, MLB rosters only, a share URL (?a=158&b=…&p=…). It is not a scoring surface, so the spoiler rule does not apply.
  • Later: reported trades. A "reported" mode for a deal you heard about. When the trade shows up on the wire, the card flips to "official" and links the transaction. No other tool does this, and it fits the second-screen use of the app.
  • Later: a third club, prospects pulled from affiliate rosters, payroll against the tax lines, competitive balance picks.
  • Skip: surplus-$ grades, paywalls, crowd-voted values.

6 · If you want to test it with people (user-research plan)

Method: 5–6 short interviews with fans who follow trade rumors, then a usability test of a wireframe.

  1. Warm-up: Which team do you follow? Where do you hear about trades first?
  2. Current habits: The last time you heard about a trade, what did you look up? Where did you look?
  3. Deep dive: Have you used BTV or the ESPN machine? When did you trust the verdict, and when didn't you? Do years of control, the 40-man roster or no-trade clauses ever come up for you?
  4. Reaction: Show the trade card with no grade. Is anything missing? Do you miss a "who wins" verdict?
  5. Wrap-up: Would you share it? Where?

What would settle it: if 4 of 6 people ask for a verdict, add a plain-language explanation. Still no single number.
