Design critique: the trade simulator's player sheet

Reviewed live on Corbin Carroll at 1280px wide, after the fix that stops the sheet clipping at the top: it now caps at the window height and scrolls inside.

Overall impression

The wide layout and the action photo make the sheet feel like a player card, a real step up from the 440px column. The weakest part is the top. The figures a trade decision turns on (projected WAR, surplus value, pay, years of control) sit in two thin rows of small mono text that read as a footnote, while the photo and the seasons table take all the visual weight.

The photo column ends a third of the way down, leaving a tall empty column beside the contract.LowMake the photo stick at the top while the right column scrolls, or put the contract and retained-salary blocks under the photo.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the action photo, then the stamped Seasons card. The photo is right for a player card. The Seasons card should rank below the value tiles, not above everything else.
  • Reading flow: name → photo → (skips the two small lines) → seasons table → contract. The two lines that summarise the trade case get skipped.
  • Emphasis: the heaviest frame on the sheet (the stamped card with its drop shadow) holds historical stats, and the forward-looking numbers have no frame at all. Reverse that weight.

Accessibility

  • Colour contrast: text is navy and caption ink on paper, which the app's contrast guard covers. The table's alternating rows hold up.
  • Touch targets: the MLB / Minors / Postseason tabs and the Keep 0/25/50% chips are full controls. The ✕ is the standard icon button.
  • Text readability: the value line's small mono text is the hardest to read on the sheet, and it is the most important text on it.
  • Alt text: the action photo carries the player's name. The header headshot is marked decorative, which is right because the name sits beside it.
  • Scrolling: fixed. The sheet now scrolls inside itself, and the page behind no longer scrolls under it.

What works well

  • The action photo gives the sheet a real sense of the player, and it disappears cleanly when there is no photo (Crisantes).
  • One seasons table with MLB, Minors and Postseason tabs answers "how has he played" without leaving the page.
  • The contract table with years and dollars is clear and scannable.

Priority recommendations

  1. Turn the value line into stat tiles under the header: Proj. WAR · Surplus value · Years of control · 2027 pay. It's the trade case, and it should be the first thing read after the name.
  2. Flatten the Seasons card (no stamp or shadow) and drop its duplicate name. The stats then support the tiles instead of competing with them.
  3. Put the add/remove action where it can always be reached: in the header, or stuck to the sheet's foot.
