Trade card header: inspiration

Today the top of /trade stacks three things that don't talk to each other. There's a bare "TRADE CARD" page title, then a boxed banner that's either an instruction ("Add a player or cash from each club…") or a blocker ("Before this trade can happen: the Brewers must clear 3 40-man spots"), then the two club bars. The title says nothing about this trade, and the banner reads like a separate notice instead of part of the deal.

What the trade tools do

ESPN Trade Machine, 2014 (archived)

A dark title band runs the full width. It holds the name, then a row of team pickers ("Team 1: Atlanta Hawks ▾ · Team 2: Boston Celtics ▾ · Add Team 3"), with Start Over at the right. The verdict is one big button under the band, "Try This Trade", and the result replaces it. The header is a control panel for the matchup.

Fanspo Trade Machine, today

A small coloured eyebrow ("Fanspo Front Office") sits over a large title. The context chips sit at the right: the season "2026-27" with the cap figure "$165.0M cap", plus Rulebook and Full width. The rules the deal is checked against are always on screen, as one compact chip.

Spotrac Trade Machine, today

A navy hero band holds the title, then three step tabs: Teams, Players and Team Needs. A sticky footer says "Choose at least 2 teams to continue" next to a Build a trade → button. What's missing is told as the next step, not as a warning box.

Seen live in the browser pane on 2026-11-19. ESPN's live page today is still the 2014 tool's "How to use" screen; its layout notes come from the archived copy studied earlier (docs/trade-machine-2014-study.html).

Three directions for our header

Each mock shows the case in your screenshot, Brewers and Diamondbacks with the Brewers over the 40-man. The colours and type are close to the app's tokens, but these are sketches, not the build.

The title becomes a navy band like a broadcast lower-third, holding the two clubs and the page actions (Share, Start over). The banner shrinks to a thin status strip fixed to the band's underside, so the verdict reads as the band's own line. When the deal works, the strip turns green: "Works on the 40-man · Brewers add 8.7 WAR". Before a deal exists, it gives the next step: "Add a player from each club".

The page title names the trade itself, "Brewers ⇄ Diamondbacks", with a small eyebrow over it. The banner becomes three chips at the right: the 40-man check, in clay when it fails; the payroll swing; and the count. Nothing is boxed, and the club bars below carry the colour. This is the quietest option and the most "stat sheet".

The header is laid out like the score header of a game page: the two clubs face each other, and the middle cell holds the verdict where the score would be ("Not yet", "Works", "Add players"). It answers "who gets what, and can it happen" in one glance. The club bars below can then drop their duplicated numbers.

My pick

A, the broadcast band. It's the same family as the 2014 Trade Machine bars you already chose for the club cards. It gives Share and Start over a home, which today sit inside the deal card. It also keeps the verdict one line tall. C is the strongest idea if you want the header to carry the deal's figures, but it repeats the club cards right below it.
