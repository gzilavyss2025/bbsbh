Can MLB clubs trade draft picks?

Mostly no. Under the current labour agreement, the only picks a club can trade are its Competitive Balance picks: Round A, which comes after the first round, and Round B, which comes after the second. Picks in every other round cannot be traded.

The rules on a Competitive Balance pick

  • A pick can be traded once, and only by the club that was awarded it. The club that receives it can't trade it on.
  • It can't be traded for cash alone.
  • Picks are awarded the winter before the draft and can be traded from then until the draft, both in the offseason and up to the July deadline.
  • Some picks that sit in those rounds still can't be traded. One case is a first-round pick dropped into Round A as a tax penalty (in 2025 the Dodgers, Yankees and Mets). Another is a compensation pick for a pick the club failed to sign.
  • Each year that's roughly 15 picks, about 7 in Round A and 8 in Round B, spread across smaller-market and lower-revenue clubs.

Possible change: Baseball America reported that MLB proposed making every pick tradable in the current labour talks, mostly from the 2028 draft. I could not confirm where that stands.

Is there an easy way to tell which picks a club has?

SourceWhat it gives
MLB.com draft order page (mlb.com/draft/2026/order)The full order, with Competitive Balance picks marked. MLB publishes it once the December lottery is done.
MLB Trade RumorsEach winter and deadline it runs a "which picks can still be traded" post that lists who holds one.
statsapi /api/v1/draft/{year}Checked live. It returns rounds labelled 1, PPI, CB-A, 2, SUP-2, CB-B…, each pick with its team. For 2026 it has 7 CB-A and 8 CB-B picks. But /draft/2027 comes back empty today: the order isn't loaded until the draft gets close. So it's good for history and not for the next draft.

What it means for the trade card

A "draft pick" chip would only ever apply to the ~15 Competitive Balance picks. For the next draft, the list would have to come from the order MLB publishes after the December lottery, because statsapi doesn't have it yet. A small nightly generator could read /draft/{next year} and use it as soon as it fills, with a hand-kept list until then.
