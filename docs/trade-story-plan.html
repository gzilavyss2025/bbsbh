A "news story" for every simulated trade

A subagent who is both a programmer and an MLB beat writer read the repo and planned a cookie-cutter story about the deal, written the way a beat writer would file it. It edited nothing. I checked its key data claims against the files: the award rows for Misiorowski (2026) and Carroll (2023, 2025, 2026) are in awards-history.json, and postseason-history.json has the Brewers (158) as 2026 champions.

The subagent found no 2026 Arizona row in run-differential.json, so the Diamondbacks' record would come from the standings or the team-records file. Worth checking why it's missing.

What to add

  • src/api/team/runDifferential.js: a memoized reader plus a pure clubSeason(data, teamId, season).
  • A pure honorsOf(playerId, awards, allStars) returning [{key, season, league}]. No new MLB call for 2022 onward; older honors come through the batched person read.
  • The final standings for last season (a live call that already exists) and a phrase for how the postseason ended: "won the World Series", "lost in the NLCS", "missed the postseason".

The template

  • Headline: "{Club A} acquire {star} in {n}-player deal with {Club B}". The star is the biggest name: top award, else most WAR.
  • Dek: what the other club lands, plus its best honor if there is one.
  • Lede: who got what, and why it matters (the hole it fills, from the depth chart). The "why" is dropped if there's no gap.
  • The haul: one paragraph per side: a count by role, total WAR and payroll added.
  • Player by player: the wire's line, then honors ("won the 2026 NL Cy Young", "a three-time All-Star"), contract or control, and prospect rank.
  • Team context: record, division finish and postseason result. Run differential only when it's 50 or more either way.
  • What it means: per club, the hole filled, the payroll change and the 40-man count after the deal.
  • Kicker: one of three closers, chosen by variant. For example, a reunion with a club the player once played for, or a defending-champion angle.

Every sentence is dropped when its data is missing, so nothing prints "undefined" or a guess. Numbers under ten are words, and the story says "postseason", never "playoffs".

How to code it

  • Builder: a pure src/screens/trade/tradeStory.js returns {headline, dek, paragraphs}. Each paragraph is a list of {text, playerId?, teamId?} pieces, the same shape the wire row's sentence uses, so names become links. Each sentence builder returns null when it's missing data. Sentence families go in small files (honors.js, clubs.js) to stay under the file-size limits.
  • Varied wording: 2–4 phrasings per sentence, picked with variant. Stable for one deal, varied across deals.
  • Fetching: one hook, useTradeStory, loads awards, All-Stars, run differential, standings, contracts and each player's facts in parallel. All of them are memoized, so reopening the story is free.
  • Tests: node tests with inline fixtures. A full deal produces every paragraph; removing awards, contract or club data drops exactly those sentences; no output contains "undefined", "null", "NaN" or "playoffs"; the wording is stable; small numbers are words.
  • On the page: a "Read the story" button under the wire row opens a sheet laid out as a newspaper column, with player and club links.
