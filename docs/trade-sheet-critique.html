Trade player sheet: header critique

The head of the player sheet on /trade-simulator: photo, name, award chips, four tiles, the line under them. Reviewed on Jake Bauers, desktop and phone, after the photo went to 4x. 2026-11-23.

Overall: the bigger photo gives the sheet a real face, and the four tiles are clear. The biggest problem: the photo now outweighs the name, and the line under the tiles repeats two of them.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the photo, at 156 × 208 px. Fine for "who is this", but the name beside it is the smallest heading in the sheet, so the name reads as a caption to the photo.
  • Reading flow: photo → chips (bright marker gold) → tiles. The name is skipped on the way: the gold chips out-shout it.
  • Whitespace: the name, meta and chips sit vertically centred on the photo in one row, which leaves about 400 px of empty paper to their right on desktop and a tall blank band above and below them.
  • Emphasis: the tiles give the four numbers equal weight. For a trade, Proj. WAR and Surplus value are the decision; Control and 2027 pay support them.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: navy on paper and ink on marker gold both pass in the design system's checked pairings.
  • Touch targets: the tiles are not interactive, good. The ✕ needs a check (above).
  • Readability: the meta line "1B · 31 YRS" is small display caps under a large photo; it is easy to miss.

What works well

  • The heavy rule between the head and the tiles cleanly separates "who" from "what he's worth".
  • Mono tabular figures in the tiles line up and read fast.
  • On the phone the photo above the name is a strong card-like opening.

Priority recommendations

  1. Cut the repeated line. Drop "2027 $16.0M · Signed thru 2030"; keep 2026 WAR and OPS somewhere once.
  2. Give the name its weight back. Stack name, meta and chips in a column aligned to the photo's top, name one step larger. Or take the photo to 3× (117 × 156) so it stops outweighing the column.
  3. Explain Surplus value with an InfoPopover, and make Proj. WAR and Surplus the two lead tiles.
