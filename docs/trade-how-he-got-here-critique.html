Design critique: "How he got here" on the player sheet

Reviewed live on Ashton Izzi (traded from Seattle to Arizona, July 24, 2025), at desktop width and at 375px. It is the trade drawn in place under the heading, which a turn ago was a "See the trade" button.

Overall impression

The facts are right and the two-sided picture is the right idea, but the section says the trade three times (a coded line, a date line, the wire's sentence) and then, on a phone, crushes two columns into 140px each. Names wrap onto two lines, "Now Arizona Diamondbacks" onto three, and "2026 WAR" splits from its number. It reads like a desktop table that was shrunk, not one built for the screen it's on.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the two club logos and the swap arrows. Those are fine signposts, but the player this sheet is about (Izzi) is marked only by a faint highlighter on his name.
  • Reading flow: heading → code line → date → logos → rows → totals → the wire sentence. That is four lines before any player, and the last line repeats the first.
  • Emphasis: the totals ("Total 2026 WAR 1.3") are set as large as anything in the section, but they are the least useful number here. A 2025 trade's 2026 WAR total says little about the player.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: ink and caption on paper pass. The highlighter on Izzi's name is the only "this is him" cue, and it carries no text. A screen reader gets no signal.
  • Touch targets: names are links. On mobile, the two-line wrapped names give tall but very narrow targets.
  • Readability: three-line wraps of "Now Arizona Diamondbacks" at 12px caps are the hardest text on the sheet.

What works

  • Both sides of the trade, with faces, in place. No modal, no extra tap.
  • Club logos as side headers make "who got whom" scannable at a glance.
  • The player the sheet is about is marked within his side.

On a wide screen the two sides can stay side by side, using the same one-line rows.

Priority recommendations

  1. Stack the sides on a phone, one line per player, with WAR right-aligned under one column head. This fixes the wrapping, which is the "looks bad on mobile" problem.
  2. Replace the coded line, the date line and the wire sentence with one plain sentence at the top.
  3. Mark the player in words ("this player", or his row first and set in bold) instead of with the highlighter only. Show "now [club]" only for someone who has moved since.

Follow-up: the Contract section for a minor leaguer

Built: a player with no contract on file (most minor leaguers) gets no Contract section at all, instead of "No contract on file yet." Cot's covers 40-man contracts only, and a minor leaguer's pay is the level minimum, not tracked per player.

What front offices and team sites track for a minor leaguer instead is his Rule 5 status: when his club has to add him to the 40-man or risk losing him. The rule (MLB's glossary): a player signed at 18 or younger must be protected within five seasons, one signed at 19 or older within four. That is the minor-league equivalent of "years of control" and could be one line on his sheet ("Rule 5 eligible: December 2026"). It needs his age on June 5 of his signing year, which the person feed carries (birth date), so it can be computed rather than guessed. Not built yet.
