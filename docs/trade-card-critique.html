Trade card critique

Everything built on /trade this session: the two club cards, the player row, the roster controls, the depth card, What moves and the trade sheet. I looked at it on the dev server at desktop and 375 px. 2026-11-18.

Overall: the parts are right and they look like Tally: stamped cards, mono figures, facts and no verdict. The page as a whole has stopped telling one story, though. The deal you're building, which is the point of the page, ends up under two long rosters and a depth table, and every row and roster carries more controls than a phone reader needs. The biggest win is putting the deal first and moving each roster behind a tap.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the club logos and the first player's name. They should come second; the deal summary should come first.
  • Reading flow: club card → roster (long) → other club → roster → depth → What moves. The answer comes last.
  • Emphasis: the trade sheet gets it right: a large WAR figure and a total for each side. The player rows don't: salary, control, WAR and OPS all share one small weight. Give the row one hero figure (WAR, or the sort's figure) and let the others drop back.
  • Colour: clay means a traded-in player, a depth count dropping, a 40-man crunch, and a no-trade flag. That's four meanings in one ink. Keep clay for "this deal makes something worse". Draw the pedigree in plain ink with a small kind label.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: the body text and labels follow the tokens, which the contrast guard checks. I didn't measure the clay pedigree text on the card or the green-on-mint depth cells; they look close to 4.5:1 and are worth a check.
  • Touch targets: buttons, chips, the cash field and the trade link are all 44 px. The Grid and Ladder switch and the depth club switch are too.
  • Screen readers: the notes and needs lists announce changes. The depth grid's struck old count reads as two numbers ("3 2"); add visually hidden "was 3, now 2" text.
  • Motion: every animation is off under reduced motion.

What works well

  • Facts, never a verdict. The trade sheet's two totals let a reader judge an old deal without being told what to think.
  • The pedigree line ("Trade · CWS '25 · R1 #3 '19") packs a career into one scan.
  • Depth before and after, with old counts struck through, makes the deal's effect on each organization visible at a glance.
  • Minor-league lines combined across levels, plus the level percentile, make prospects comparable with each other.
  • Motion is narrow and earned: only things the reader caused move.

Priority recommendations

  1. Deal first: a pinned summary strip (payroll, WAR, 40-man for each side), so every add shows its effect right away.
  2. Each roster in a sheet: the page becomes deal → two short club cards → depth, and the long list stops pushing everything down.
  3. One tap target per player row, opening a player sheet for the contract, pedigree, trade and retain. The row keeps name, attributes, one line of numbers and Add.
  4. One filter row per roster: search plus a Filter button.
  5. Clay means "worse" only; no stripes in the depth grid.
