Contact Replay

The at-bat replay (PR #1521) ends when the pitch reaches the plate. This study adds the swing, the contact or the miss, and the ball after contact. It also adds the quality line. The scene uses the app's real camera (scene.js: 6 ft behind the plate, 4 ft high, focal length 410), the app's paper tokens, and the app's pitch colours. All numbers on this page are invented samples, not real games.

Beat sheet for this sample · live playhead

One clock, eight beats

  • Pitch

    No change to PR #1521. The trail thickens as the ball comes closer, in the pitch's own colour. At ×3 the pitch takes about 1200 ms.

  • Coil

    Anticipation. The bat winds back a little before it fires, so the eye knows a swing is coming. Real 90 ms, shown 270 ms. Pencil.

  • Swing

    The barrel gathers speed into the ball. This is the one place an ease-in is right: the app already uses --ease-press only for things that are thrown or pushed. Real 160 ms, with 4 smear frames behind the bat.

  • Hit-stop

    On contact everything freezes for 66 ms (4 frames at 60 Hz). A whiff gets no freeze, so the missing beat itself says "miss".

  • Contact

    One ring from 0.8× the ball, never wider than one zone cell, and 8 short ink ticks, the same tick idea as the big-play stamp. The ring's size and strength follow squared-up %, so a flush hit looks flush. One flash only.

  • Exit

    The ball leaves on its measured launch angle and spray angle in navy ink, a new colour for a new object. Its trail is as wide as the ball at each point, like the pitch trail, so the departure reads. The scene follows it only until it leaves the frame. From behind the plate a grounder runs UP the screen toward the horizon, like a liner. So the measured trajectory (GROUND BALL) shows as a tag at contact, and the ball gets a shadow and a ground track while it flies.

  • Side view

    A cut to a side view at one fixed scale (0 to 450 ft) for every at-bat. The pace matches the player page's Contact card: 500 ms per second of hang time, 700 to 2800 ms. The landing ring is that card's ring.

  • Quality line

    Figures rise 4 px and fade in, 220 ms each, 55 ms apart (--stagger-step). The verdict lands last with the stamp-in curve, 380 ms.

Left out on purpose

  • A batter figure. A body would be a third kind of mark that is neither measured nor useful. The bat alone carries the swing.
  • A camera pan after a fly ball. A pan rescales the scene during a transition, which Heer and Robertson warn against. The cut to a fixed-scale side view keeps one scale per view.
  • Numbers that count up. Scorebook figures are written, not spun. A count-up also delays the one number the reader came for.
  • Bounce and confetti. The app's curves settle with no overshoot. A home run gets a second landing ring, as on the player page, and nothing more.
  • Onion-skin dots on the pitch. Some pitch tracers use them, but the app's thickening trail already reads well. The replay must not change the Now Pitching card's look.

Still open (from the issue)

  • The /gf spoiler call. /gf returns the whole game. This design reads it only inside a revealed at-bat, but where it may be held is your call.
  • Live tier only, or both tiers? Try the "Final tier" button: only the bat at contact changes from pencil to ink.
  • Card height. The side-view strip keeps its space on every pitch, so the card does not change height mid-replay (ADR-0046: nothing moves while it plays).
  • MiLB. No tracked flight means no replay, as today. A park with flights but no bat data gets the default swing.
