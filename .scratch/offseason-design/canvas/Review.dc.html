Review notes

Two reviewers read concepts A, B and C. One read for hierarchy and wording. One read for whether the numbers were sound. Both reviews are applied on page 1.

What changed
Headline
The 2026 season is in the books
A+ OFFSEASON. MLB OFFSEASON at MLB. The head names the level and the state, and stops. The deck that followed it is cut: the level tabs, the date banner and the card below already carry everything it said.
Ledger strip
1,980 games · 30 clubs · SEP 6
1,980 was 30 clubs × 132 scheduled games ÷ 2. It was the schedule multiplied out, not a count of games that exist. Seasons lose games to weather, to seven-inning doubleheaders, and to feeds too thin to score. The strip is cut and no count is shown.
Phone layout
On all three original phone boards the primary action sat below 844px. The club strip stays where it is today, so the eyebrow and the deck came out instead. Score this game now lands near 650px, above the fold.
Notebook figure
The largest number on the note was one player’s rate, on a page about nine players. It is now the report’s own count over its pool: 14 of 187.
Which note
“The patient hitters” is replaced by youngest regulars. A 250-PA floor at one level excludes the players who were promoted in June, so the old note selected for players who stayed. Age against the level has no such bias.
Standings
The table and the bracket come off the home page. The table showed 3 of 6 clubs on a phone, which hid every club below .500. The series counts were set in the same type as W-L and needed a footnote to say they were not scores.
Cut
“A player to meet” (a name and a sentence, with no line, age or org). “Note 01 of 12” (12 notes do not exist). The three-control note footer. “Deal another”, replaced by filter chips. The padlock glyph on the seal band: the tape already means sealed.
The one disagreement
What leads the minor-league page
Reviewer 1
The game card. It is the only concept that ends with the reader scoring a game, which is what the app is for.
Reviewer 2
A random game from June is not a reason to open the app in December. A minor-league follower follows players moving between levels.
Resolved
The game card leads and carries a reason line: 7 players in this game reached the majors. That is a fact about careers, not about this game, so it spoils nothing. Players who moved up runs second. Both use data the app already ships: milb-alumni/ records who reached the majors, and minors-leaders.json carries each player’s level path and parent club.
Two versions

Both reviewers reached the same split. At the minor levels there is almost no news in winter, so the page offers a game. At MLB the winter is the busiest news period of the year, so the page reports it.

Identical at every level
The shell and its behaviour. The archive invitation drawn as a slate game card, not a button. Kraft tape means one thing everywhere: results are behind it. The gate line: Opening this shows results. The scoring label: Score this game.
Open decisions
1
When does this page replace the slate? A level holds three leagues that finish on different dates, and the postseason runs on after them. Per league, or only when every league at the level is done? A finished club is not a finished league.
2
Is October part of the offseason? The Arizona Fall League plays through October and November with players from these levels. Probe statsapi for it before building the empty state.
3
Build the season record for MLB and link out for the minors? MLB standings and postseason pages already exist. The minors need half-season qualification rules that are not validated, including whether AAA runs two halves in 2026.
4
How is the game pool checked? No count is shown any more, but every game dealt still has to be scoreable. A game with a thin feed and no lineups is a dead end at the one moment the page asked for a tap.
