# Decisions for Gary — cards and section heads (#1113)

Six questions. Only you can answer them. Each one has a recommendation. If you
agree with all six, say "all six as recommended" and the work can start the day the
pill work (#1131) is done.

Some words used below:

- A **card** is a box of paper with a thin edge and rounded corners. The team page
  has about 16 of them.
- A **band** is the coloured bar at the top of a card or section, with the thick line
  under it. On a club's page it takes the club's colours.
- A **label** is the small grey heading in capital letters with a thin line under it.

---

## Q1. Can all cards use one of two shapes?

Today the app draws cards in four shapes. Two of the differences are only a shadow.
On the manila paper you cannot see that shadow. The corners are the part you can see:
10px round or 6px round.

**Recommendation: yes. Two shapes only.**

- **Sheet** — the round 10px corners. 86 cards.
- **Ledger** — the tighter 6px corners. 25 cards.

23 cards get a shadow that they do not have today, and 4 cards lose one. On paper
you will not see either change. If a screenshot shows that you CAN see it, we stop
and add a third shape.

## Q2. When a page has no club colour, what does a band look like?

Today there are two answers:

- The game pages (lineups, innings, box score) show a **navy band with a kraft line**
  when there is no club colour.
- The team page and player page show a **plain grey label** when there is no club
  colour. The band appears only when the club colour arrives.

**Recommendation: keep both, exactly as they are today.** The new heading gets one
switch for this. Nothing on screen changes.

The other choices: make every band navy (the team page changes for clubs without
colours, mostly minor-league clubs), or make every band a plain label (the game
pages lose their navy bars).

## Q3. Can the box score's sheets change to card paper?

Six boxes in the box score (pitchers of record, player of the game, the insights,
the Statcast box, the stars, the inning tally) sit on the same paper colour as the
page. Every other card in the app is a slightly lighter,
creamier paper. On a phone the difference is small but visible.

**Recommendation: yes. Use card paper, like every other card.** You will see the box
score's boxes become a little lighter. They will then match the cards on the rest of
the app.

## Q4. Boxes inside boxes: fix them now or later?

26 small boxes sit inside a card. Examples: the "win call" and "umpire favour" boxes
inside the at-bat stat box, the roster list inside the roster card, and the grey
wells inside the minor-league tiles. A box inside a box is the look that makes a page
read like a business dashboard and not a scorebook.

**Recommendation: later.** This work keeps them as they are and lists them. A
separate issue decides, box by box, which ones to flatten. Doing it now makes each
change bigger and harder to undo.

## Q5. Do the lab and admin pages get the new card?

25 card-shaped boxes are on tool pages that only you use: the wordmark lab, the
identity lab, the pattern lab, the animation lab, the admin copy editor and the
contract review queue. The admin pages cannot be tested on a laptop, because they
need your sign-in.

**Recommendation: no, leave them.** They are tools, not the product. The one
exception is the design lab: it shows the new card and heading so you can look at
them before each change.

## Q6. Do report cards get their own look?

The Season Report card on the team page and the "Who challenges" chart are built like
business reports: a score out of 10, a card inside a card, a chart with a trend line.
The design review asked whether these should get their own "report" look.

**Recommendation: not now.** They take the normal sheet shape in this work. If you
want a report look, it is one new shape added later, after you see the rest of the
app on the new cards.
