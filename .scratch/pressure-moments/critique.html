Pressure Moments — design critique

The one-board redesign of /pressure-moments, checked live at 375 px and desktop width.

Overall impression

The single board fixes the old problem: one table, one question at a time, and the club strip reads as the obvious scope control. The biggest remaining issue is the phone: three full-width filter menus come before the first at-bat.

Visual hierarchy

  • What draws the eye first: the club strip and the stamped card label ("Milwaukee Brewers batting"). Right: they say what the table is.
  • Reading flow: strip → label → Batting/Pitching → filters → rows. On desktop it's one tidy band; on a phone the filters break the flow.
  • Emphasis: the pressure meter carries the page, as it should. The ▶ in solid ink is heavier than the "What happened" text it sits next to; that's fine, but 50 of them make a strong right-hand column.

Accessibility

  • Contrast: ink on paper and white ▶ on ink both pass comfortably.
  • Tap targets: switch 44 px tall and menus 40 px pass; the ▶ (24 px) fails.
  • Readability: rows wrap to two lines at 375 px and stay readable; the table doesn't scroll sideways (339 of 339 px).

What works well

  • One table answers one question; the stacked three-card scroll is gone.
  • The card label always names exactly what you're looking at.
  • Filters carry across club changes, so "this pitcher against my club" is two taps.

Priority recommendations

  1. Fold the filters on phones into a "Filter" button and sheet, so row 1 sits above the fold.
  2. Grow the ▶ tap area to 44 px with the box score's ::after pattern.
  3. Label the MLB pin as "All" so the scope switch is clear before the tap.
