Wireframe concepts — layout only, no visual design

Farm Digest

Two structural concepts for a "what happened today across the system" page, plus the small notation system used throughout. Boxes stand in for real content; every number and name below is illustrative, not a real published stat line. Grayscale and system fonts throughout on purpose — this is about where things sit and how big they are, not what they look like.

All three concepts carry the same underlying content (scores, top performers, key moments, pitching outliers, ABS challenges, trending prospects, a "who to know" primer, org-wide leaders, transactions, game stories, AAA bullpen health, the positional depth chart, and system-wide Farm Index) — Concepts A and B differ only in how the DAILY content is arranged; Concept C is reference content pulled onto its own page rather than the daily scroll.

Concept A

Digest Scroll

One continuous scroll, top to bottom by importance — a KPI band, then the single biggest story, then trending, then each affiliate in proximity-to-majors order, then the slower-moving context (bullpen health, moves, Farm Index) at the bottom. Best for a reader who scrolls once and is done.

Concept C

Depth Chart

A reference page, not a daily one — organizational strength by position. Scouting rank and current performance are kept as two separate lists on purpose: FanGraphs' own Board does the same, and no outlet has a published formula for blending a scouting grade with a stat line into one number. Reached from the teaser on either digest concept.

All three concepts carry annotations (small italic monospace lines) marking which convention a block is borrowing from — a KPI band ceiling of ~5 metrics, hierarchy by size/position/isolation rather than color, arrow+delta+sparkline together for trend, a two-line headline-plus-detail row for the moves ticker, progressive disclosure ("Show N more") reused from this app's own existing pattern, and Concept C's split scouting/performance lists borrowed directly from FanGraphs' Board rather than inventing a blended score nobody else publishes. Two blocks — the game story and the pitcher stuff-percentile line — are shown here to establish where they'd sit, not because either is a guaranteed day-one build: the recap needs a new off-device scrape, and the percentile needs no new data (this app already ingests AAA pitch-tracking) but does need its own comparison logic. Nothing here is a final layout — the point is to compare the bets before any of it gets designed.
